## 2. Research Background & Motivation

Automated Language Assessment (ALA) and Computer-Assisted Language Learning (CALL) systems are rapidly transforming foreign language education. However, existing automated assessment engines (such as standard automated essay scoring and spoken response grading) face significant limitations:

1. **Superficial Feature Overfitting:** Traditional scoring models rely heavily on surface metrics (e.g., word count, sentence length, keyword overlap) rather than semantic coherence, grammatical subtlety, and multi-turn argumentation.
2. **Lack of Actionable Pedagogical Feedback:** Standard testing engines output a rigid numerical score (e.g., IELTS Band 6.5) without providing contextual, granular guidance on *how* the learner can rectify phonological, lexical, and syntactic errors.

Bridging my undergraduate background in language linguistics (University of Dhaka) with computational systems at KCGI, I designed and deployed an end-to-end multi-modal language evaluation system (`AIwithIELTS`). While this demonstrated successful practical orchestration of speech-to-text transcription (Whisper) and rubric parsing, it highlighted the need for formal academic research into **rubric alignment, multi-modal phoneme evaluation, and hallucination-free pedagogical feedback**.

---

## 3. Core Research Questions

1. *How can we align Large Language Models with complex standardized scoring rubrics (e.g., IELTS/TOEFL/CEFR criteria: Grammatical Range & Accuracy, Lexical Resource, Coherence & Cohesion) to achieve high inter-rater reliability with expert human examiners?*
2. *How can acoustic speech features (phoneme duration, pause distribution, pitch variance) be unified with lexical transcripts to assess non-native oral fluency without penalizing diverse non-native regional accents?*
3. *How can we construct structured, hallucination-free feedback loops that generate personalized language acquisition exercises based on detected error patterns?*
